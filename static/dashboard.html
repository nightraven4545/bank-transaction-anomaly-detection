      How a payment becomes a case

      1. The payment arrives

        Date, agency, merchant, category, amount. No cardholder is named.

      2. Features put it in context

        Higher always means more unusual. The dot is this payment; the grey band is the middle half.

      3. Three detectors score it

        In standard deviations above a typical payment. #1 is the most unusual.

      4. One model sets the order

        Share of real frauds caught at 1%. LOF breaks on repeated identical payments and drags the average down, so Isolation Forest ranks the queue.

      Limits

      What the results don't show

      • The card data is from 2013 and hides its fields.
      • DC and Czech have no labels: an alert is unusual, not proven wrong.
      • Planted frauds encode our own idea of fraud.
      • Accounts with few payments have noisy baselines.

      How it should be used

      • A flag is a reason to look, never a block.
      • No age, occupation or wealth inputs.
      • Uploads are scored in memory and never stored.
      • The queue is sized to the team, not to a threshold.
